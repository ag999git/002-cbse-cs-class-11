# Types of computers, and a short history

**Beyond syllabus** · not in the CBSE syllabus · Chapter 1, section 1.1

## Types of computers by size and use

| Type | What it is | Example of use |
|---|---|---|
| **Supercomputer** | The fastest computers, with thousands of processors working together | Weather forecasting; India's PARAM and Pratyush supercomputers |
| **Mainframe** | Large computers that serve thousands of users at once | Banks, railway reservation |
| **Server** | A computer that provides a service to other computers over a network | Websites, e-mail, school result pages |
| **Desktop and laptop (personal computers)** | One user at a time | Home, school, office |
| **Tablet and smartphone** | Small, touch-based, battery-powered | Calls, apps, photos, payments |
| **Embedded computer** | A small computer built into a machine to do one job | Washing machine, car, ATM, lift |

## A short history

| Years | Step forward | What it changed |
|---|---|---|
| 1940s | Computers built with **vacuum tubes** (e.g. ENIAC) | Room-sized machines that broke down often |
| 1950s–60s | **Transistors** replace vacuum tubes | Smaller, cooler, more reliable |
| 1960s–70s | **Integrated circuits**: many transistors on one chip | Computers the size of a cupboard |
| 1970s onward | The **microprocessor**: a whole CPU on one chip | Personal computers |
| 2000s onward | Laptops, smartphones, the internet everywhere | A computer in every pocket |

The ideas behind every one of these machines are the same as in Chapter 1: input, process, output and storage, a CPU
that follows a stored program, and a hierarchy of memory.
