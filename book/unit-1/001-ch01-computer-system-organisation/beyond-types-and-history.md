# Types of computers, and a short history

**Beyond syllabus** · not in the CBSE syllabus · Chapter 1, section 1.1

Every computer in this page, from a room-sized machine of the 1940s to the phone in your pocket, follows the same plan
as in Chapter 1: input, process, output and storage, with a CPU that follows a stored program. What changed is how small,
fast and cheap the parts became.

## Types of computers by size and use

| Type | What it is | Example of use |
|---|---|---|
| **Supercomputer** | The fastest computers, with thousands of processors working together | Weather forecasting, climate study, design of medicines; India's PARAM and Pratyush supercomputers |
| **Mainframe** | Large, very reliable computers that serve thousands of users at once | Banks, railway reservation, insurance |
| **Server** | A computer that provides a service to other computers over a network | Websites, e-mail, school result pages, UPI payments |
| **Desktop and laptop (personal computers)** | One user at a time | Home, school, office |
| **Workstation** | A powerful personal computer for heavy work | Video editing, engineering drawings, 3D animation |
| **Tablet and smartphone** | Small, touch-based, battery-powered | Calls, apps, photos, payments |
| **Embedded computer** | A small computer built into a machine to do one job | Washing machine, car, ATM, lift, microwave, smart TV |

*Did you know?* There are far more embedded computers in the world than all other kinds put together: a modern car alone
has dozens.

## Types of computers by how they work

| Type | How it works | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Digital** | Works with numbers made of 0s and 1s | Every computer you use |
| **Analog** | Works with quantities that change smoothly, such as voltage or pressure | An old car speedometer; slide rule |
| **Hybrid** | Both: measures with analog parts and calculates with digital ones | Hospital machines that measure heartbeat and then analyse it |

## Before electronic computers

| When | Device | What it did |
|---|---|---|
| Thousands of years ago | **Abacus** | Beads on rods for adding and subtracting; still used in mental-maths classes |
| 1642 | **Pascaline** (Blaise Pascal) | A machine of gears that could add and subtract |
| 1830s | **Analytical Engine** (Charles Babbage) | Designed, never finished: it already had a "mill" (the CPU) and a "store" (memory). Babbage is called the father of the computer |
| 1840s | **Ada Lovelace** | Wrote steps for the Analytical Engine: often called the first computer program |

## The generations of computers

| Generation | Years (roughly) | Main part | What it changed | Example |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| First | 1940s–1950s | **Vacuum tubes** | Room-sized machines that used a lot of power, got hot and broke down often | ENIAC (USA, 1946) |
| Second | 1950s–1960s | **Transistors** | Smaller, cooler, cheaper, more reliable | IBM 1401 |
| Third | 1960s–1970s | **Integrated circuits (ICs)**: many transistors on one chip | Computers the size of a cupboard; keyboards and monitors | IBM System/360 |
| Fourth | 1970s–today | **Microprocessors**: a whole CPU on one chip | Personal computers, laptops, phones | Intel 4004 (1971), today's laptops |
| Fifth | Today and ahead | **Artificial intelligence**, many processors working together | Computers that understand speech, recognise faces, translate | Voice assistants, AI chatbots |

*Hook:* **T**ubes, **T**ransistors, **I**Cs, **M**icroprocessors, **A**I.

## Computers in India

| Year | Milestone |
|---|---|
| 1956 | The first computer in India, HEC-2M, is installed at the Indian Statistical Institute, Kolkata |
| 1960 | **TIFRAC**, the first computer designed and built in India, at the Tata Institute of Fundamental Research, Mumbai |
| 1991 | **PARAM 8000**, India's first supercomputer, built by C-DAC, Pune |
| 2018 | **Pratyush** at the Indian Institute of Tropical Meteorology, Pune, for weather and climate forecasts |
| 2016 onward | UPI makes India a world leader in digital payments, run on large networks of servers |

## Questions with answers

**Q1.** Name the main electronic part of each generation of computers.

**Answer.** First: vacuum tubes; second: transistors; third: integrated circuits; fourth: microprocessors; fifth: artificial intelligence (many processors).

**Q2.** What is an embedded computer? Give two examples.

**Answer.** A small computer built into a machine to do one job; for example, in a washing machine and an ATM.

**Q3.** Who is called the father of the computer, and why?

**Answer.** Charles Babbage, because his Analytical Engine (1830s) already had a mill (a CPU) and a store (memory) and could follow a program.

**Q4.** Name India's first supercomputer and its maker.

**Answer.** PARAM 8000 (1991), by C-DAC, Pune.

**Q5.** Which kind of computer is used for railway reservation, and why?

**Answer.** A mainframe, because thousands of users book tickets at the same time and the system must be very reliable.

**Q6.** Differentiate between digital and analog computers.

**Answer.** A digital computer works with numbers made of 0s and 1s; an analog computer works with quantities that change smoothly, such as voltage.
